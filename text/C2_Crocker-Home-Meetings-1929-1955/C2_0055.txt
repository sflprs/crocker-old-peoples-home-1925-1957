May 20, 1942.

ANNUAL MEETING

OLD PEOPLE'S HOME

Pursuant to notice, duly given, the Annual Meeting of the corporation
Imown as the Old People's Home wes held this day at the Home, 2507 Pine Street,
San Francisco, California, at 2:00 o'clock peme

After checking the mmber of members present (17), it was found that
there was not a quorum present. However, with Mre De Je liurphy, Vice President,
presiding, the meeting was called to order. Mrs. Re Co Burling acted as
Chairmane “

Mrse Leavitt, President of the Board of Lady Managers, read her report,
which was approved and ordered filed; and submitted her resignation, which on
motion, duly made and seconded, was ordered filed.

Mrse Josephine Borda, Secretary of the Board of Lady Managers, sub-
mitted her report, and on motion, duly made and seconded, same was approved and
ordered filede

The revort of the Treasurer of the Board of Lady Managers was read by
Mr. Hicklin, and on motion, duly made and seconded, was ordered filed.

Mr. Hicklin, the auditor for the Hom, then read his report am also
the report of Fletcher Ge Flaherty, the attorney for the Homee After a general
discussion both reports were approved and ordered filed.

The election of officers was then called fore Mr. Murphy stated that
inasmuch as there was no quorum, the election of officers at this time would not
be legale He also stated that the Trustees were unable to present candidates
and suggested that this be taken up at a special meeting to be called. Mr.
Murphy stated that it wes the duty of the Lady Managers to help select Trustees
and said that he would gladly accept any suggestions they might make in this
regard.

Mr. Hicklin then read a letter from the Internal Revenue Department
with reference to the exemption of the Home from Income, Social Security and
Capital Stock taxes. Mrs. Leavitt suggested a rising vote of thanks to Mr.
Hicklin and Mr. Flaherty for the good work they did in securing these exemptions.

After a general discussion regarding the procedure for accepting new
members for the duration of the war, and Mrs. Leavitt's experience in trying to
obtain a permit to operate the new infirmary from the City and County of San
Francisco, there being no further business, the meeting adjourned.
